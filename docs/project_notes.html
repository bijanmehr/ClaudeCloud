1Scope

The work started with one task. The task was to design a clamp for a cracked wood cylinder. The clamp must hold the crack closed so that the crack does not become longer. The clamp must use PETG and TPU together. The clamp must not use screws or other separate fasteners.

During the work, the scope became larger. A test print showed that TPU does not bond well to PETG. This problem is important for future robot parts, for example rover frames and drone frames. In these parts, PETG is the strong material and TPU is the flexible material. Thus this note also tells you how to connect PETG and TPU so that the joint does not fail.

3What we found

3.1PETG and TPU do not bond well

  • A flat PETG–TPU joint is weak. We found no published value for this joint. Published tests on PLA, ABS and ASA joints with TPU show 0.4 to 6.4 MPa. This is about 1.5 to 23 % of the strength of TPU 95A (about 27 MPa).
  • A joint with a mechanical lock is much stronger. Published tests show a strength of about 5 to 9 MPa. Most of these tests used other materials, not PETG and TPU.
  • The first clamp lining had 5 dovetail keys. The keys went through the full height. Thus the lining could move up and down. Only the adhesion held the lining in that direction.
  • A vertical joint is better than a horizontal joint. In a vertical joint, the two materials are side by side in each layer.
  • Heat at the joint makes the bond better. In one test, a hotter TPU nozzle (190 → 220 °C) made a TPU lap joint on PETG 5.6 times as strong. This test used a foaming TPU, and it is a preprint without peer review. At 240 °C, the joint was weaker again. The foam is a possible cause. The Bambu TPU 95A HF profile already uses 230 °C.
  • We could not confirm most of the published values in this section from the original papers. Use your coupon results for design, not these values.
NOTE

The Bambu settings can cool the joint too much, but we did not measure this. From the second layer, the TPU profile sets the part fan to 100 %. On the H2D, it also sets the auxiliary fan to 100 %. When TPU is in the job, the printer cools the chamber more than for PETG. Before each nozzle change, the printer heats the idle nozzle to 10 °C below the print temperature. Thus each TPU line can go onto cold PETG.

3.2Beam interlocking (Bambu Studio)

Bambu Studio can interlock the two materials at the joint. The name of the setting is "Use beam interlocking". The slicer cuts the joint into small beams, half PETG and half TPU. The direction of the beams turns 90° after some layers. Thus the joint locks in all directions.

  • The setting is in Process > Others > Advanced. Bambu Studio shows it only when the Advanced switch is on. You can also set it for one object only. To do this, right-click the object and select "Edit Process Settings".
  • Bambu Studio makes the beams only inside one object. The beams go between areas that have different wall filaments: parts, painted areas or filament modifiers. Bambu Studio does not make beams between two objects that touch.
  • The beams do not go near the outer surfaces. With the default values and 0.2 mm layers, a joint less than 2.4 mm high has no beams. A joint narrower than about 4.5 mm also has no beams. From 4.5 to 5.5 mm, the result changes with the position of the joint on the slicer grid. A rim of about 1 to 2.5 mm at the edge of each joint has no beams. A peel starts at this edge.
  • The interlocking zone goes 1 to 3 mm into each material. In a thin TPU part, this zone can put PETG beams into TPU that must stay soft.
  • The beams have the same width in the two materials. TPU is the weak side.
  • Published data shows that interlocked joints have a strength of about 5 to 9 MPa. Most of these tests used other materials. The best PETG + TPU value that we found is 8.6 MPa. This value is from eSUN, the filament manufacturer, not from a research test. It is for 1.6 mm beams, depth 5, in the XY direction.
CAUTION

Do not leave a gap between the PETG body and the TPU body. A gap of 0.01 mm stops all beam interlocking. This is an error in Bambu Studio: the code does not convert a unit.

Make the faces touch exactly. You can also make the faces overlap by 0.2 to 0.5 mm. Then put the TPU part after the PETG part in the Objects list. Bambu Studio cuts the overlap out of the earlier part, so the later part keeps its full shape.

NOTE

A lower boundary avoidance does not repair a gap. With a gap, the Preview can show beams near the joint, but no beam crosses the gap. The gap problem occurs only between side faces. A 0.05 mm gap between a top face and a bottom face had no effect in our test.

Beam interlocking makes the inner area of a joint stronger. It does not make the edge of the joint stronger. Thus use it together with a mechanical lock. Do not use it as the only lock.

3.3A lock by shape

A lock by shape holds the TPU when the adhesion is zero. We wrote a test for this. The test moves the TPU body in 26 directions. If the TPU can move more than 2 mm in one direction, the lock is not satisfactory. The test keeps the TPU rigid, but soft TPU can bend and come out. Thus, a pass is necessary, but it is not enough.

  • Old clamp lining: the TPU can move up and down. Not satisfactory.
  • New clamp lining: the TPU cannot move more than 0.15 mm in any direction. Satisfactory.

The new lining has these lock features:

  • PETG lips at the top face and the bottom face. The lips go over the TPU edge.
  • PETG dovetail ribs that go into the TPU. The ribs have gaps along their height. The TPU fills these gaps.
  • PETG end walls at the two ends of the lining.
  • A thicker TPU skin (3 mm; the old skin was 1 mm). On the TPU side, the interlocking zone stays in this skin, at least 0.65 mm from the fin roots.
CAUTION

Do not use the capture test as the only test of a lock. Soft TPU can bend and come out of a shallow undercut before it breaks.

Use locks that make closed loops: TPU through PETG holes, TPU around PETG pins, or TPU flanges inside PETG. Add PETG stops that keep the movement small. If the TPU connects two PETG parts that move, do the capture test for each PETG part alone. Then do a pull test on a printed part.

3.4Mechanism design

  • You can print a hinge already assembled. The hinge gaps must agree with the layer height. We used 3 empty layers (0.6 mm) and a side clearance of 0.45 mm.
  • Set supports off. Supports fill the hinge gaps. Also put a support blocker around each moving joint in the 3MF.
  • Set thick bridges off. Thick bridges make the hinge gaps smaller. Do not change the layer height or the first layer height (0.2 mm).
  • The first layer above a hinge gap is a bridge. If this bridge has only a small anchor, it can bend down and bond to the part below. Then the hinge cannot turn. Put a 45° chamfer under each pin hole. Set the bridge fan to 100 %.
  • Put the prime tower away from moving joints. A TPU string across a 0.45 mm gap does not break when the hinge turns.
  • A split ring on soft springs bends at the split if the ring is not stiff. The PETG wall of the clamp is 6 mm thick for this reason.
  • A cam that goes 10° past the dead centre locks the lever. The load holds the lever closed.
  • One change can cause a problem in a different area. A larger hinge pin (6 mm to 8 mm) made the cam and its clearance cut larger. This change made a sharp notch where the knuckles join the arm. A full check after each change found the notch.
  • Wood is not soft like a hose. Thus the clamp must have a spring between the PETG and the wood. TPU fins are this spring.
  • Keep the strain in a TPU flex hinge at about 25 % or less. The strain is the fold angle (rad) × web thickness / (2 × free web length). For a 90° fold, make the free length 3 times the web thickness or more (hybrid_lib/blocks/flex_hinge.scad).
  • Print a TPU web flat on the bed. Then the bending strain is along the extruded lines, not across the layers. Printed TPU is weakest across the layers.
  • A solid TPU 95A pad is very stiff in compression. It prevents slip, but it gives almost no travel. Use the shape to make the spring: thin webs that bend, spokes, lattices or torsion cords. The calculation for the isolation mount (hybrid_lib/blocks/iso_mount.scad) shows that TPU 95A does not isolate vibration below about 200 Hz.

3.5Long-term behaviour

  • We found no fatigue data for PETG–TPU joints. Almost all published joint data comes from tests that pull each joint one time until it fails. Thus these values are too high for a part that bends many times each day.
  • TPU becomes softer during the first load cycles. After that, it stays stable. Bend a new TPU spring 10 to 20 times before you measure it.
  • After a long compression, TPU 95A does not fully go back to its initial shape. At room temperature, it keeps 30 to 38 % of the compression. At 70 °C, it keeps 45 %. These values are from BASF and Essentium data sheets, not from Bambu. Thus a TPU part that is always compressed loses some force with time. The TPU fins of the clamp can lose some clamp force.
  • PETG has a low fatigue strength. Keep the stress in the PETG low. Put all the movement that repeats into the TPU.
  • Use these start values for a part that bends many times. They are estimates, not test results. The cyclic stress in the PETG must be less than 5 MPa. The cyclic stress in the TPU must be less than 3 to 5 MPa. The stress at the joint must be less than 20 to 25 % of the coupon result.
  • Put a fillet of 1 mm or more at each TPU root. Put a fillet of 1 to 2 mm or more at each PETG notch near the TPU.
  • The coupon flex test (200 cycles) is only a first test. Before you use a suspension part, bend one part 10 000 times or more through the real movement. Then examine the joint for a gap or a peel.
  • The bed is at 70 °C. Joints near the bed stay warmer during the print. If possible, put the most important joints in the first 5 to 10 mm of the part.

3.6Analysis and test

  • We used a sequence of checks: motion, 3D collision, forces and stress (FEA with CalculiX).
  • A very high stress at one node is not always real. A sharp inside corner gives an infinite value in a linear model. Do a second calculation with a different mesh. If the peak moves or becomes much lower, it is a mesh error. If the peak stays and increases, remove the sharp corner with a fillet, if possible. If not, read the stress 0.5 mm from the corner.
  • Two FEA peaks had different causes. The first peak was a real sharp notch, and we removed it. The second peak was a corner effect, and we kept the shape.
  • A model is not a test. The hinge test print showed the bond problem. No calculation showed it.

3.7Slicer and files

  • For two materials, supply two STL files: one file for each material. Import the two files at the same time. Then answer "Yes" to "Load these files as a single object with multiple parts?".
  • If you answer "No", or import the files one at a time, the parts move. The TPU is then in an incorrect position.
  • Bambu Studio first sets each part to filament 1. Set the TPU part to filament 2 with "Change Filament". Bambu Studio shows this menu only when the project has two or more filaments.
  • The 3MF file does not set the nozzle. Use "Edit filament grouping" on the plate. Put PETG on the left nozzle. Put TPU on the right nozzle.
  • Make sure that the project has two filaments before you open the 3MF. If the project has only one filament, the TPU part prints in PETG. The slicer does not show a warning.
  • After you set the filament of each part, do not change the filament on the object row. This change puts the two parts on one filament again.
  • Feed the TPU 95A from the external spool, not through the AMS. Do not use Bambu "TPU for AMS" (68D) for these parts. It is much harder than TPU 95A, and we designed the parts for TPU 95A.
  • If you open a 3MF from another program, Bambu Studio shows the message "The 3mf is not from Bambu Lab". This message is normal.
  • Settings for one object in a 3MF stay only when you open the file as a project. "Import geometry only" deletes them.
  • A PrusaSlicer 3MF must have the name "PrusaSlicer" in its Application data. If the 3MF does not have this name, Bambu Studio loads all parts as one part.

4What to do better

  1. Do a small physical test first. Print a joint test before the full design. We found the bond problem late. A coupon print on the first day can show this problem.
  2. Agree on the requirements first. The first design used six M3 bolts. The first request did not include a rule about screws. After we saw this design, we added the requirement "no screws". Write the requirements in a short list. Agree on them before the design starts.
  3. Measure before you write a statement. One statement about beam interlocking was incorrect. A measurement with the Bambu code found the error. Use the real tool, not memory.
  4. Make sure that the real slicer loads the file correctly. At the start, we only read the Bambu Studio source code. Later, the compiled loader found an error in the PrusaSlicer 3MF. The Bambu 3MF was correct, but we did not write the loading steps (filaments and nozzle groups). Do the loader test at the start. Write the loading steps at the same time.
  5. Show results early and often. Long calculations ran for many minutes without a message. Give a short status. Show a picture before the full checks.
  6. Know the limits of the cloud computer. It had 4 CPUs and 15 GB of memory. A tetgen mesh from the STL used all the memory, and the job stopped. A rebuild of the part as an exact solid in gmsh solved this problem. One FEA run stopped when other large jobs ran at the same time. Run large FEA jobs one at a time.
  7. Do not let the analysis scripts give old or incorrect results. Make the scripts read the dimensions from the model. We typed some dimensions into two scripts by hand, and these scripts gave incorrect results after a design change. Always show the error output. One build failed without a message and left an old report.
  8. Do the full sequence of checks after each change. A small change caused a problem in a different area.
  9. Ask for the reference model at the start. The network blocked MakerWorld. The STL file from the user solved this problem.
  10. Calibrate the models with tests. The fin stiffness and the hand force are calculated values. Measure them on a printed part. Then correct the models.
  11. Make the TPU sections thick where the beam interlocking must go. Design the part for the interlocking zone from the start. Do not add it at the end.
  12. Do a separate review before you make the print files. A separate review of the tolerances and the print risks found three problems that our checks did not find. The slide-on clearance was too small. Supports could fill the hinge gaps. The first layers above the hinge gaps had almost no support. Tell the reviewers to find faults. Make a second agent examine each problem that the reviewers find.

5Procedure for a new PETG + TPU part

  1. Write the requirements: loads, movement, size, and the parts that you must not use.
  2. Give each material its function. PETG takes the load. TPU makes the part flexible.
  3. Design each PETG–TPU joint with a lock by shape. Use one of these four lock types. The four types are TPU around a PETG anchor, TPU rivets through PETG holes, dovetails, and PETG lips over the TPU.
  4. Keep the joint in compression or shear. Do not let a joint peel.
  5. Put the joint faces vertical, if possible. Then the two materials are side by side in each layer.
  6. Keep the TPU in as few layers as possible. Each layer with PETG and TPU needs a nozzle change. The full clamp has about 350 nozzle changes, because the TPU lining goes through the full height. Each nozzle change adds print time and prime tower material.
  7. Make the PETG and TPU faces touch exactly. Do not leave a gap.
  8. Make the TPU near the joint 3 mm thick or more. If this is not possible, make the interlocking zone small.
  9. Do the capture test (hybrid_lib/tools/capture_check.py). The TPU must not move more than 2 mm in any direction.
  10. Load the files in Bambu Studio as one object with two parts. Set the filaments and the nozzle groups.
  11. Set the beam interlocking settings to the start values in section 6. Examine the beams at the joint in the Preview.
  12. Print the joint coupons with the same filaments, temperatures and fan values as the part. The coupon 3MF has its own interlocking values (45°, depth 3, boundary avoidance 0). If the part uses different values, change them on coupons B and H. Do the pull, peel and flex tests.
  13. Print the part. Do a test with the real load. Write the results in this note.
CAUTION

Do not use the adhesion between PETG and TPU as the only lock in a part that flexes many times. The joint can fail after some cycles.
